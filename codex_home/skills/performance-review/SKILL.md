# Performance review

## Inputs

The manager's notes or draft, the review period, the role, and the rating scale and review cycle from the business's policies (read_policy if one is listed). If the business has none, use a simple 3-level scale (Below / Meeting / Above expectations) and say so. If there are no notes, ask for 3-5 concrete examples before writing.

## Output

1. **Summary** (3-4 sentences): overall performance against the role's expectations in the period.
2. **Achievements**: 3-5 bullets, each a specific example with impact ("reduced the backlog from 120 to 40 tickets by ...").
3. **Development areas**: 2-3 bullets, each describing an observable behaviour or result, its impact, and what good looks like. No labels about personality.
4. **Rating rationale**: which rating on the scale the evidence supports and why. Present it as a suggestion; the manager decides.
5. **Goals for the next period**: 3-4 SMART goals.
6. **Development plan**: 2-3 actions (training, stretch assignment, mentoring) with timing.
7. **Wording check** (for the manager, not the employee): list any phrases from the notes you changed and why.

## Bias and fairness rules

- Describe behaviour and results, not personality. Replace words such as "abrasive", "aggressive", "emotional", "bossy", "not a culture fit", "lacks presence", "high-maintenance" with the specific behaviour and its impact, or ask the manager for an example. Flag them in the wording check.
- Never refer to age, gender, family or carer's responsibilities, health, disability, pregnancy, parental or other leave taken, part-time or flexible work, ethnicity, accent or religion. Leave taken under the NES or the business's policies must not count against the employee.
- Use the same standard of evidence for positives and negatives. If the notes are mostly negative with no examples, say the review needs evidence before it is shared.
- If the notes describe possible misconduct, a health issue, or conduct that could be bullying or harassment, stop and recommend getting advice (the adviser in the business profile, an employment lawyer, or the Fair Work Infoline 13 13 94) before the review goes ahead.
