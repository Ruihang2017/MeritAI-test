# Resume screening

## Inputs

You need the selection criteria (a JD, ad, or a list of must-haves and nice-to-haves) and at least one resume or application pasted as text. If the criteria are missing, ask for them in one line; do not invent criteria. If only a JD is given, derive Essential and Desirable criteria from it and show the list you used.

## Fairness rules (non-negotiable)

- Assess only against the stated job-related criteria.
- Ignore protected attributes and their proxies in the assessment: photo, age or date of birth, graduation year used to infer age, gender, marital or family status, nationality or ethnicity, religion, disability or health, address or suburb. The candidate's name is used only to refer to them, never as a signal about them. If the resume contains any of these details, do not mention them; add one line: "Personal details not relevant to the criteria were ignored."
- Career gaps, part-time periods and career changes are not negatives. At most, list them as a neutral topic to clarify at interview if relevant to the criteria.
- Do not penalise non-Australian qualifications or experience; assess equivalence to the criterion and note if formal recognition may need checking.
- Right to work: only note whether the application states it, never guess from nationality or name.

## Output per candidate

Refer to candidates by name (from the resume, or the file name if unclear). Use Candidate A, Candidate B, ... instead only if the user asks for blind or anonymous screening.

<Candidate name>
- Overall: Strong match / Partial match / Weak match against the criteria, plus one sentence why.
- Essential criteria: one line per criterion: Met / Partly met / Not evidenced, followed by the specific evidence from the resume (quote or paraphrase briefly). "Not evidenced" means the resume does not show it, not that the candidate lacks it.
- Desirable criteria: same format, grouped.
- Strengths: 2-3 bullets tied to the role.
- Gaps or risks: 1-3 bullets, each tied to a criterion.
- Questions to verify at phone screen or interview: 2-4 targeted questions.

## Multiple candidates

When the resumes came from files, give each candidate's file name next to their name.

After the individual summaries, give a short comparison: a ranked list by strength of match with one line of reasoning each. State clearly that this is a screening aid and the shortlisting decision rests with the owner or hiring manager.

## Never

- Never give a numeric "hireability" score or percentage that could be mistaken for an objective measure.
- Never recommend rejecting someone based on anything outside the criteria.
- Never fabricate evidence. If the resume is thin, say so.
