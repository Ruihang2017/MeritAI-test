# Onboarding plan

## Inputs

Role, team, manager, start date, location or work arrangement, and anything special (licences to verify, equipment, systems access). Use placeholders for anything missing: [Start date], [Manager], [Buddy], [Location]. Use the business profile and, if listed, the business's own onboarding or induction policy (read_policy). Do not invent the business's systems, tools or people. Keep it realistic for a small business (the owner or one manager usually does all of it).

## Output

1. **Pre-start checklist** (owner and due date for each item, counted back from the start date). First call new_starter_checklist for the employment type (if the user did not say, assume full-time, say so in one line, and do not stop to ask; set is_apprentice_or_trainee for an apprentice or trainee) and include all its legal items with their links (information statements, TFN declaration, super choice, right to work). Then add the practical items: contract signed and returned, payroll set up, uniform, keys, equipment and system logins, site induction booked, buddy assigned, welcome message sent.
2. **Day one**: a time-blocked schedule (arrival and who meets them, induction including WHS, systems setup, lunch with the team or buddy, meeting with the manager to go through the role and the 30/60/90 plan).
3. **Week one**: day by day, mixing training, shadowing, introductions to key stakeholders, and one small real task by the end of the week.
4. **30/60/90-day goals**: 2-4 goals per stage, specific and observable, tied to the role. Include the check-in meetings (e.g. end of each stage) and link them to probation if the business profile or policies define it.
5. **Welcome email** to the new starter (only if asked or clearly useful): start time and place, who to ask for, what to bring, first-day agenda in brief, a friendly line from the manager. Sign off with the signer from the business profile, or [Your name].

## Rules

- Use inclusive, practical language. Ask about adjustments: include a line inviting the new starter to share any adjustments they need.
- Do not include personal details about the new starter beyond name, role and start date.
- Keep it realistic: no more than the new starter can absorb in week one.
