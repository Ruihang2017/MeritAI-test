# Interview kit

## Inputs

Role and selection criteria (JD or list). Optional: interview format (phone screen, panel, site visit), length (default 45 minutes), specific concerns from screening to probe, number of interviewers.

## Structure of the kit

1. Opening (2-3 min): a short script to welcome the candidate, introduce the panel, explain the format and that notes will be taken.
2. Competencies: pick 4-6 competencies from the criteria (e.g. Customer service, Problem solving, Stakeholder management, Attention to detail). For each:
   - 1 main behavioural question in STAR form ("Tell me about a time when...").
   - 2 follow-up probes (e.g. "What was your specific role?", "What would you do differently?").
   - What a strong answer includes: 2-3 bullets of observable indicators.
   - Warning signs: 1-2 bullets.
3. Role-specific technical or situational questions: 2-4, relevant to the actual work described in the role (e.g. for a customer service role: handling a customer disputing a charge; for a software role: debugging a production incident). For roles with physical, on-site or safety-critical work, include at least one safety question.
4. Candidate questions and close (5 min): script for inviting questions, explaining next steps and timeline [Timeline].
5. Scoring guide: a 1-5 scale defined once (1 = no evidence, 3 = meets the requirement with a clear example, 5 = exceeds, multiple strong examples with impact), then each competency listed with space to note evidence and a score. Remind interviewers to score independently before discussing.

Phone screen variant (15-20 min): confirm interest and availability, right to work (ask the same question to every candidate: "Do you have the right to work in Australia for this role?"), salary expectations, notice period, 2-3 key criteria questions, and any screening questions from resume-screening.

## Question rules

- Ask every candidate the same core questions for the same role.
- Never ask about: age, family plans, pregnancy, childcare, marital status, religion, ethnicity or origin ("Where are you really from?"), health or disability in general, union membership, criminal history (unless there is a lawful, role-specific check process; then refer to that process rather than asking ad hoc).
- If an inherent requirement matters (e.g. working at heights), ask about the ability to perform the task, with or without adjustments, not about health conditions.
- Keep questions open-ended; avoid leading questions.
