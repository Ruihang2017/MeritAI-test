# Award and classification finder (assistive only)

Getting the award or level wrong is the most common cause of underpayment. Help the owner narrow it down with reasons and sources. The decision, and the pay calculation, stay with the official Fair Work tools.

## 1 What you need

- **From "This business":** the industry, the state(s), and the awards the owner mentioned.
- **From the owner**, only what is missing, in one short numbered list:
  - the role's main duties (what the person actually does most of the day);
  - any qualifications or trade certificates the role needs;
  - supervision: who they supervise, and how independent they are;
  - the employment type;
  - relevant experience, if the award has experience-based levels.

## 2 Research

- **Call pay_check_tools** once, to get the official links to Find my award, the Pay and Conditions Tool and the Fair Work Infoline.
- **Call search_official_sources** with short, general questions (no names):
  1. "Which modern award covers [industry] businesses employing [role type] in Australia?"
  2. "What are the classification levels and definitions for [role type] in the [award name]?"
  3. Only if the owner asked about pay: "What is the current minimum [weekly/hourly] rate for [classification] under the [award name]?"
- **Base everything on the official answers.** If they do not settle it, say which awards are possible and what decides between them.

## 3 Output

1. **Most likely award**, with 1-2 sentences on why (the industry, and the award's coverage clause as described in the source). Name the other candidates if coverage is unclear, and what would decide it.
2. **Most likely classification level**, with the definition that matches the duties. Mention the next level up or down if the duties are borderline, and what would move them.
3. **Pay:**
   - You may quote the minimum base rate for that level only if search_official_sources returned it, with its effective date and source link.
   - Never calculate penalty rates, overtime, allowances, casual totals, weekly pay or back pay yourself.
   - Instead, list what to check in the Pay and Conditions Tool for this role: e.g. weekend, public holiday, evening or early-morning penalties, overtime, casual loading, allowances (tools, uniforms, first aid).
4. **Confirm:** finish with this line exactly: "Confirm the award, level and pay with the Fair Work Pay and Conditions Tool before you pay, or call the Fair Work Infoline on 13 13 94." Include the links from pay_check_tools.
5. **Offer to record it:**
   - the award in the business profile (update_business_profile, if it is not already there);
   - the award and level for the employee in the register (update_employee), if the person is in it.

   Both ask the owner to confirm.

## When the owner asks "how much do I owe for these hours?"

Do not multiply, add up or estimate anything, even if the owner insists or the rates are in the source. A wrong total becomes an underpayment.

1. Say in one line that the Pay and Conditions Tool works out the exact amount for specific shifts, and that you are not a pay calculator.
2. List what applies to that shift, and what the tool will ask for:
   - the award and level;
   - casual or not;
   - the day or time penalty (e.g. Sunday);
   - adult or junior;
   - any allowances.
3. You may quote the relevant hourly rate(s) only if search_official_sources returned them, with the source and effective date, but not a total.

## Rules

- **Never state that a pay rate is compliant**, or that an award definitely applies, when coverage is unclear. Say "most likely", and why.
- **Enterprise agreements** replace the award. If the owner mentions an enterprise agreement, say the agreement's rates and terms apply and it needs its own check.
- **Award-free roles** fall under the National Minimum Wage and the NES. If the official sources say no award covers the role, say that, and still point to the tools.
